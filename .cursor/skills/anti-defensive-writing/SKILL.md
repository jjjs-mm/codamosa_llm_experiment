# Anti-Defensive Writing

## Core rule

Advance the claim directly. State what the evidence shows, what the text argues, what the method does, and where the result applies. Do not lead with hypothetical objections or repeated disclaimers.

Use a calm, competent authorial posture. Write as an author explaining an argument to the reader, not as an author negotiating with an imagined critic.

## Preserve necessary precision

Keep limitations when they affect validity, interpretation, scope, research design, safety, or the reader's ability to use the result correctly. State each necessary limitation once, clearly and in the appropriate section.

## Revision procedure

1. Identify the sentence's function: claim, scope condition, methodological limitation, conceptual contrast, evidence-based qualification, or redundant clarification.
2. Delete disclaimers that add no evidence, scope, logic, or necessary guidance.
3. Convert defensive limitations into positive scope. Prefer: `The analysis focuses on ...` over `We do not claim that ...`.
4. Replace vague hedging with precise uncertainty. Name the source of uncertainty, such as sample size, design, or measurement.
5. Rebuild each paragraph around one clear topic sentence and one main job.

## Preferred patterns

- This paper examines ...
- This study shows ...
- The analysis focuses on ...
- The evidence indicates ...
- This design allows ...
- The results suggest ...
- The central contribution is ...

## Avoid unless necessary

- This paper does not claim ...
- We do not attempt to ...
- This is not to say that ...
- This should not be taken to mean ...
- To be clear ...
- Although this study has limitations ...
- It is worth noting that ...

## Final pass

Remove sentences that mainly protect against hypothetical misunderstanding rather than advancing the argument. Keep real methodological limits, but express them with precise scope and evidence rather than apology, repetition, or self-undermining language.
